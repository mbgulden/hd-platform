## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need kayaking experience for Oahu kayak tours?

**No experience needed for most tours.** Sit-on-top kayaks are wide, stable, and nearly impossible to flip in calm conditions. Self-guided tours (Active Oahu, Hawaii Beach Time, Tsue's Farm) include a 5-10 minute safety briefing covering paddling technique, navigation, and what to do if you capsize. Guided tours (KBA, TwoGood) have instructors in the water with you.

If you've never kayaked, we recommend starting with Tsue's Farm (flat river, zero risk) or a guided tour with Kailua Beach Adventures.

### What is the best time of year for kayaking on Oahu?

**May through October** offers the calmest conditions on Oahu's windward (east) side where most kayak tours operate. Winter months (November–March) can bring larger north and east swells — tours still run but may reroute or cancel on rough days.

**Best time of day:** Morning (8–10am departure). Winds typically pick up after noon, especially on the windward side. Morning paddles also have better visibility for snorkeling and spotting turtles.

### Can I kayak to the Mokulua Islands on my own?

**Yes, but with caveats.** The Mokulua Islands (Moku Nui and Moku Iki) are about 1.5 miles offshore from Kailua Beach — a 45-60 minute paddle each way in good conditions. You can paddle there with a self-guided rental from Kailua Beach Adventures or Active Oahu Tours.

However, only Kailua Beach Adventures and TwoGood Kayaks hold the **state landing permits** required to legally step onto Moku Nui (the larger island, which is a seabird sanctuary). Without a guided tour through these operators, you can paddle around the islands but cannot land.

**Safety note:** The channel between Kailua Beach and the Mokuluas can get choppy when winds pick up. Go early, check conditions, and don't attempt alone if you're a beginner.

### What should I bring on an Oahu kayak tour?

**Essentials (every tour):**
- Reef-safe sunscreen (zinc-based — Hawaii bans oxybenzone/octinoxate sunscreens)
- Water (at least 1 liter per person)
- Hat with a strap (wind will take it)
- Sunglasses with a strap (same reason)
- Water shoes or secure sandals (flip-flops WILL float away)
- Waterproof phone pouch or dry bag for valuables

**Nice to have:**
- Rash guard or quick-dry shirt (sun protection while paddling)
- Waterproof camera or GoPro
- Snacks (especially for half-day tours)
- Change of clothes in the car

Most operators provide dry bags for phones/keys. Check our full packing guide: [What to Bring Kayaking on Oahu →](/guides/what-to-bring-kayaking/)

### Is kayaking on Oahu safe?

**Yes, with basic precautions.** Thousands of visitors kayak Oahu's waters safely every month. Key safety points:

1. **Sit-on-top kayaks** used by all tour operators are extremely stable
2. **Life jackets (PFDs)** are provided and required
3. **Guided tours** have CPR-certified guides who monitor conditions
4. **Self-guided tours** include safety briefings and route guidance
5. **Morning paddles** avoid afternoon wind chop

**What to watch for:**
- Afternoon trade winds (especially on windward side) — go in the morning
- Sun exposure — you'll burn faster on the water. Reapply sunscreen frequently
- Dehydration — paddle with water accessible in your kayak's storage well
- Winter north swells (November–March) — check conditions before booking

### Can I see sea turtles while kayaking?

**Yes, especially on the windward side.** Green sea turtles (honu) are common around Kailua Bay, Kaneohe Bay, and near the Mokulua Islands. They frequently surface near kayaks and rest on reefs. **Best chances:** Morning tours, May–September, with Kailua Beach Adventures or TwoGood Kayaks (guides know where turtles hang out).

Turtles are federally protected — maintain 10+ feet distance, never touch or chase them.
